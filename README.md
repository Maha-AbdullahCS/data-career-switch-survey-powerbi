# Data Career Switch Survey — Power BI Dashboard
## About the Project

An interactive Power BI dashboard analyzing survey data from professionals who transitioned into different data-related career fields. The project explores career backgrounds, salaries, job roles, programming languages, and other factors related to careers in data.

## Tools
- Power BI
- Power Query

## Data Source

The dataset was originally provided by Alex The Analyst for educational purposes and is available through his GitHub repository.

[Original Dataset & Resources](https://github.com/AlexTheAnalyst/Power-BI/tree/main)

## Data Preparation

The dataset was cleaned and prepared using Power Query before being used to build the Power BI dashboard.

Key preparation steps included:
- Removing unnecessary columns
- Handling missing and null values
- Correcting data types
- Cleaning and standardizing text values
- Preparing the data for analysis and visualization

## Dashboard

The Power BI dashboard provides an interactive overview of the survey data, allowing users to explore different aspects of careers in data-related fields through interactive visualizations and filters.

The Power BI dashboard file is included in this repository:

`Data_Career_Switch_Survey_PowerBI.pbix`

  ## Key Insights

### 1. Demographics & Overview

- The survey includes **630 respondents** with an average age of **30 years**, representing a relatively young group of professionals and aspiring data professionals.

- The **United States** accounts for the largest share of survey respondents, followed by **India, Canada, and the United Kingdom**, while respondents from other countries collectively represent a significant portion of the sample.

### 2. Salary & Job Roles

- **Data Scientists** have the highest average salary among the job titles shown, with average compensation approaching **100K**.

- **Data Engineers** and **Data Architects** follow, with average salaries around **60K–65K**.

- **Database Developers** and respondents categorized as **Students/Looking/None** are at the lower end of the average salary distribution.

- Average salaries for **female and male respondents are relatively close** in this sample, showing only a small difference between the two groups.

### 3. Breaking Into Data

- Breaking into the data field appears to be challenging for many respondents.

- **42.7%** of respondents rated the difficulty as **"Neither easy nor difficult"**, making it the largest response category.

- **24.76%** considered breaking into data **"Difficult"**, while **4.29%** rated it **"Very Difficult"**.

- Approximately **28%** of respondents considered the transition **easy or very easy**, suggesting that entering the data field can present a significant challenge for many career changers.

### 4. Career Satisfaction

- The average **work-life balance satisfaction score is 5.74/10**, indicating a moderate level of satisfaction.

- The average **salary satisfaction score is 4.27/10**, which is noticeably lower than satisfaction with work-life balance.

- This difference suggests that although respondents report moderate satisfaction with their work-life balance, they are generally less satisfied with their compensation.

### 5. Programming Languages

- **Python** is the most prominent programming language among survey respondents, followed by **R** and other languages.

- Python and R appear across multiple data-related job roles, highlighting their broad relevance within the data field.

- **Data Analysts represent a large share of respondents across the programming-language categories**, while language preferences vary depending on the respondent's career path.

### 6. Career Switching Into Data

- A significant number of respondents transitioned into data-related careers from **other professional backgrounds**, making "Other" the largest industry category shown.

- **Technology, Finance, and Healthcare** are also strongly represented among respondents who switched careers into data.

- Across almost all industries shown, more respondents answered **"Yes"** to switching careers into data than **"No"**, highlighting that data careers attract professionals from a wide range of backgrounds.

### 7. Overall Findings

- The survey demonstrates that **data-related careers attract professionals from diverse educational and professional backgrounds**.

- While data careers can offer strong earning potential—particularly for roles such as **Data Scientist, Data Engineer, and Data Architect**—entering the field can still be challenging.

- The relatively low **salary satisfaction score of 4.27/10** compared with the **5.74/10 work-life balance score** suggests that compensation may be an area of concern for many respondents.

- Overall, the results highlight the growing appeal of data careers while also showing the challenges professionals may face when transitioning into the field.
